When Trump entered office in 2017, businesses bearing his name continued to operate. That created an unusually concrete way to study the intersection of the presidency and private commerce: a customer could pay a Trump property while simultaneously doing business with the United States government. The public record shows that foreign-government spending at Trump-associated businesses became the subject of congressional investigation and constitutional litigation. It does not establish that every payment bought a policy decision.

House Oversight Committee Democratic staff documented **at least $7.8 million** from 20 foreign governments to four Trump businesses in the accounting records they reviewed. Those records covered only a subset of the business empire. A separate review by Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington estimated **$13.6 million** using a broader evidence base. These are competing scopes, **not figures to add together**. The congressional analysis raised emoluments questions, but the applicable litigation did not conclude with a trial verdict establishing that every documented transaction was illegal. Another paper trail concerns public money. Secret Service personnel used Trump-linked properties while providing protection, producing lodging bills and questions about pricing, necessity and appropriate separation from presidential financial interests. Political committees and campaign organizations also spent money at Trump properties. Those are measurable expenditures. Whether an individual payment crossed a legal line requires records about the transaction and applicable law, not a blanket conclusion drawn from its destination. [C011](/cases/C011.html) [C076](/cases/C076.html)

The distinction matters throughout the dossier: knowing where money went is often easier than knowing why an official decision was made. Reporting cannot bridge that gap by treating temporal proximity as proof of a bargain. It can, however, identify precisely which contracts, communications, ethics waivers or beneficiary records are missing.
