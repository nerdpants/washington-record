Some of the clearest legal findings in the record are about control of appropriated money rather than private enrichment. The Government Accountability Office concluded that withholding certain Ukraine security assistance during Trump's first term violated the Impoundment Control Act. During the second term, GAO identified statutory violations concerning designated federal programs, including the Institute of Museum and Library Services, FEMA funds and federal health-research grants. These are determinations about agency compliance with appropriation law; they are not automatic criminal convictions of named officials. [C015](/cases/C015.html) [C208](/cases/C208.html) [C209](/cases/C209.html) [C210](/cases/C210.html) [C211](/cases/C211.html)

The dossier also contains significant cautionary counterexamples. GAO did not find an Impoundment Control Act violation in every pause it reviewed, and found certain acting FEMA leadership appointments lawful. A publication that omits these narrower findings would distort the record, even if its purpose were only to document failures. [C212](/cases/C212.html) [C213](/cases/C213.html)

DOGE provides another example. GAO and other reviewers raised questions about financial-disclosure and ethics controls, access to agency systems and calculations used to advertise savings. A reported savings number is **not the same as audited money saved**. The archival entries distinguish documented deficiencies and accounting disagreements from allegations that any particular official stole data or committed a crime. [C164](/cases/C164.html) [C165](/cases/C165.html) [C166](/cases/C166.html)

The oversight process itself became contested in disputes over inspector-general access, agency officials' compliance with courts, and record-retention duties. Each episode calls for its own order, response and appellate status rather than an omnibus verdict on everyone involved. [C170](/cases/C170.html) [C206](/cases/C206.html) [C219](/cases/C219.html)
