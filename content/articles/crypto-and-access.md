A second-term investigation cannot be understood using hotel rooms and real estate alone. The Trump family's participation in digital-asset ventures created new revenue channels, investor relationships and regulatory questions. World Liberty Financial filed a federal securities offering notice in October 2024 describing a proposed offering far larger than the amount actually sold as of that filing. Treating the offering ceiling as revenue would produce a fundamental accounting error. Subsequent filings and financial disclosures require the same careful separation between gross proceeds, related-party payments and personal income. [C030](/cases/C030.html) [C031](/cases/C031.html) [C032](/cases/C032.html)

A token-holder dinner offered access to the president to prominent participants. Reporting also connected investments in World Liberty with regulatory developments affecting some crypto-industry figures. Such arrangements raise questions about the sale of access and perceived conflicts; those questions cannot be answered by assuming every token purchase was a personal payment to Trump or that every agency action resulted from a transaction. [C033](/cases/C033.html) [C034](/cases/C034.html)

One of the largest figures in the dossier is **$2 billion**, describing an Abu Dhabi-backed investment in Binance that World Liberty said would be settled using its USD1 stablecoin. The investment was **into Binance**, not a $2 billion purchase of World Liberty or an equal payment to the Trump family. Determining any economic gain to stablecoin affiliates requires separate information about reserve assets, fees and ownership. Another reported $500 million foreign-linked stake arrangement concerns a different transaction and should not be merged into the first. [C035](/cases/C035.html) [C036](/cases/C036.html)

The government also shifted digital-asset enforcement policy and addressed corporate cases and pardons involving people in the industry. An April 2025 Justice Department memorandum set out a new enforcement approach. A policy change can benefit private companies without itself proving corrupt favoritism. A defensible account looks for concrete conflicts, departures from stated agency criteria, and communications between those seeking relief and the decision-makers. [C037](/cases/C037.html) [C038](/cases/C038.html) [C040](/cases/C040.html)
