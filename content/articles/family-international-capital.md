A major question in the period between Trump's terms is what former public officials did after leaving office. Jared Kushner, the former senior White House adviser and presidential son-in-law, launched Affinity Partners. Congressional records describe a **$2 billion commitment** from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund despite objections recorded by investment staff. The amount was investment capital committed to a firm, not a $2 billion personal cash payment to Kushner. The records raise questions about post-government relationships and fund economics; they do not, by themselves, prove bribery. [C028](/cases/C028.html)

Former Treasury Secretary Steven Mnuchin separately obtained Gulf-linked investment capital for Liberty Strategic Capital. Those transactions should be studied alongside Kushner's investment, but never combined as if they were payments to a shared beneficiary. Understanding management fees, ownership interests, regulatory decisions and any official communications is more informative than adding headline dollar figures. [C029](/cases/C029.html)

A much-discussed transaction from the first term concerns Kushner Companies' 666 Fifth Avenue property. Brookfield entered a 99-year lease in 2018 for a reportedly approximately **$1.28 billion** prepaid arrangement. Brookfield had Qatar-linked investors elsewhere in its business, yet said the particular transaction was financed by a different fund without that participation. The public dossier therefore does **not** establish direct Qatari funding of this lease. That qualification is central, not a footnote. [C012](/cases/C012.html)

There were other international points of contact: trademarks sought by Ivanka Trump-linked businesses in China; foreign real-estate development announcements after the 2024 election; and business relationships involving partners in the Gulf and Asia. Government approval of a trademark or a development permit is a fact to record. Showing an improper exchange for U.S. policy requires much more: the decision-maker, the timeline, the benefit, the policy act, and persuasive evidence connecting them.
