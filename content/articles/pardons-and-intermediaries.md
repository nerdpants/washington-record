Few presidential powers invite a sharper distinction between **lawful authority** and **potential improper influence** than clemency. A pardon may forgive federal offenses; a commutation changes a sentence without necessarily removing a conviction. The Constitution gives presidents broad discretion. That authority does not remove legitimate questions when lobbyists, donors, relatives and other intermediaries appear around a particular decision. The case archive separately indexes those episodes rather than implying that clemency itself is wrongdoing. [C025](/cases/C025.html) [C064](/cases/C064.html) [C176](/cases/C176.html) [C177](/cases/C177.html)

One prominent second-term case involves **Henry and Imelda Cuellar**, pardoned in December 2025 while facing federal charges arising from alleged payments linked to foreign interests. Both denied wrongdoing, and there was no criminal trial verdict establishing their guilt. The administration's public treatment of the pardon and later political commentary warrant examination, but neither a pardon nor political rhetoric establishes a corrupt exchange. [C134](/cases/C134.html)

A more complicated financial network surrounds the pardon of banker **Julio Herrera Velutini**. Reporting described a proposed employment arrangement involving Trump lawyer Alina Habba, political giving by the banker's daughter and lobbying by Ballard Partners. An official lobbying disclosure reported **$630,000** for fourth-quarter 2025 banking-regulatory lobbying. A reported employment package exceeding $50 million is a proposed contract value, not verified cash paid. Habba and the White House denied she arranged the pardon. Those are facts and contested accounts that belong together in the same case file. [C130](/cases/C130.html) [C131](/cases/C131.html) [C132](/cases/C132.html) [C133](/cases/C133.html)

The expanded archive includes clemency for people convicted of financial crimes, political corruption and drug-related offenses, including **Ross Ulbricht**, whose sentence was ended by a pardon, and **Garnett Gilbert Smith** and **Larry Hoover**, whose federal sentences were commuted. The distinction between a pardon and a commutation is legally significant, as is the absence of verified proof that any one of these particular drug-related decisions was purchased. Other entries document reported lobbying fees, donor relationships or questions about victim restitution on a case-by-case basis. What a credible review can say is that the clemency system creates opportunities for advocacy and access that are difficult for outsiders to audit. It cannot responsibly transform a donor's proximity or a paid lobbyist's activity into proof of an illegal bargain without the missing link. The online archive therefore gives every clemency case its own sources, legal action and caveat.
