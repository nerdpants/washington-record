Donors do not disappear from political life once an administration takes office. The second-term archive examines donations and pledges tied to White House construction, companies with federal business, fundraising organizations, media settlements and political causes. Several entries concern the proposed ballroom project and its roster of private supporters. Reports about **pledged** contributions are not proof that the same amount was collected in cash, and a donor's government contracts do not automatically mean a purchase of influence occurred. Media-company settlements and commercial projects add another layer. The record contains a Paramount/CBS settlement, other litigation payments, a planned presidential-library financing structure and a documentary licensing agreement associated with Melania Trump. They are distinct commercial, legal and political transactions. Their relevant terms, decision-makers and distribution of proceeds must be checked independently. The Justice Department's **Anti-Weaponization Fund** illustrates why later developments matter. The department announced a proposed $1.776 billion mechanism in May 2026, then rescinded the establishing order in August. The later order stated that no members were appointed, no claims process created, no funds transferred and no claims paid. Calling it a functioning multibillion-dollar payout program after that rescission would be inaccurate. [C091](/cases/C091.html)
