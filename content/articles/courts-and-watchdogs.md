Some episodes rest on more than suspicion. New York authorities obtained a **$2 million court-ordered payment** concerning misuse of charitable assets by the Trump Foundation. The Trump Organization's two corporate entities were convicted of criminal tax offenses and fined **$1.61 million**; Trump himself was not a charged defendant in that corporate tax prosecution. In 2024, a New York jury convicted Trump on 34 felony business-records counts; the January 2025 sentence was an unconditional discharge, without incarceration, probation or a fine. These separate outcomes must not be blended into one legal allegation. [C002](/cases/C002.html) [C004](/cases/C004.html) [C006](/cases/C006.html)

The New York civil-fraud case requires particular care. An intermediate appellate court in August 2025 **vacated a large monetary award** while otherwise affirming portions of the civil judgment. A reader encountering the earlier dollar amount without its later appellate disposition could walk away with a materially false understanding. Its procedural history remains distinct from the criminal tax verdict and the 2024 business-records conviction. [C001](/cases/C001.html)

The formal oversight record extends well beyond Trump's own companies. A federal inspector general found that **20 of 21** trips reviewed for former Health and Human Services Secretary Tom Price failed applicable travel requirements, with at least **$341,000** in federal funds described as wasted. The Office of Government Ethics detailed omissions and inaccurate statements involving Commerce Secretary Wilbur Ross's financial disclosures, while distinguishing those ethics concerns from proof of a criminal conflict-of-interest violation. These are different types of findings, but both show why inspector-general reports and ethics correspondence are important starting points. [C024](/cases/C024.html) [C023](/cases/C023.html)

For other officials, the record is more contested. The investigation of Elaine Chao's ties to family shipping interests and ethics reviews of Interior Secretary Ryan Zinke require attribution to the offices that conducted them, along with their documented limits. Being investigated, retaining a business asset, or having a family member with a commercial interest does not by itself establish criminal wrongdoing. [C022](/cases/C022.html) [C097](/cases/C097.html)
