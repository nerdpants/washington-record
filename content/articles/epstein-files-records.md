A late addition to this archive concerns the federal handling of records related to Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell. Congress enacted the **Epstein Files Transparency Act on November 19, 2025**, calling for disclosure of covered records subject to statutory exceptions, including protections for victims. The Justice Department announced a release totaling nearly **3.5 million pages** in January 2026 and described a larger body of potentially responsive material. A numerical difference between records reviewed and records published does **not** by itself establish destroyed evidence or illegal concealment. [C220](/cases/C220.html) [C221](/cases/C221.html)

Court proceedings offer a more precise picture. In **Phang v. Blanche**, a federal district court issued a preliminary injunction in June 2026 requiring the government to disclose or explain specified redactions, address underlying interview notes, review certain foreign-language material and satisfy publication requirements. In September, the court accepted several redactions on victim-protection grounds while ordering further review of other items; it declined to hold officials in contempt at that stage. These are meaningful judicial findings, but not convictions for perjury, obstruction or trafficking. [C222](/cases/C222.html) [C223](/cases/C223.html) [C229](/cases/C229.html)

A report by **Democratic staff of the House Judiciary Committee** in October 2026 presented serious allegations involving Trump, Paolo Zampolli and other people, drawing on interviews and federal records. Trump and the White House denied the allegations. The staff's account is not a bipartisan committee adjudication or a judicial finding of criminal conspiracy. Similarly, published records of Blanche's interview with Maxwell can be examined, and critics can question the interview's direction, without asserting that a court found witness tampering. [C224](/cases/C224.html) [C225](/cases/C225.html) [C226](/cases/C226.html)

The July 2025 DOJ–FBI memorandum, an announced inspector-general audit and a later Federal Register report belong in that chronology as well. The report rejects unauthenticated claims from a user-supplied supplement about supposedly missing alphanumeric evidence caches and deliberately purged financial ledgers. Those assertions had no substantiated chain of custody in the provided document. [C227](/cases/C227.html) [C228](/cases/C228.html) [C229](/cases/C229.html) [C230](/cases/C230.html)
