# Presidential Administrations: Documented Conflicts, Oversight Findings and Reported Improprieties

## Independent editorial and evidentiary review — Edition 7.1 | 31 October 2026

**Scope:** First Trump administration (2017–2021), relevant between-term dealings, and second administration through 31 October 2026. This is a documented research inventory, **not a finding that every named person committed misconduct**.



## Executive findings and reading guide

This edition reorganizes and preserves all 219 previously indexed entries, but moves the 43 unresolved research leads to a distinct appendix. Some entries concern different aspects of the same transaction, some are methodology-only and some involve conflicting accounts; therefore **219 is not a count of proven crimes, separate incidents or culpable people**.

**Evidence codes:** P — a cited primary record supports a *limited factual proposition*; R — reliable named reporting with underlying records not completely audited; M — mixed or disputed documentary support; U — open lead only. The codes are source-support classifications, NOT guilt or corruption ratings.

**Audit reach:** Every row received a structural/editorial screen: source ID reconciliation, link presence, grade and claim type, monetary-label review, duplicate-theme reconciliation and publication safeguards. Selected high-impact propositions received new external checks. The remainder retain earlier sourcing and are **not independently re-verified line by line**.

**Financial method:** Contract ceilings, valuations, pledges, investment flows, assets, government obligations and restitution orders are never added as if they were equivalent cash receipts. A verified government action followed by a reported donation does not prove an illegal exchange.

**Approach:** Both the underlying allegations and material responses are preserved. Courts, auditors, agency announcements, newsroom investigations and partisan oversight letters are separately attributed. Evidence may evolve after the cutoff.

**Current inventory:** 230 indexed entries (219 original plus 11 verified-limited supplementary case entries) — P 62, R 82, M 32, U 43; 230 source-register records, including additions for post-announcement legal developments.

## 1. Court dispositions, investigations and formal determinations

This chapter distinguishes court findings, settlements, indictments and official actions. A criminal conviction, civil judgment, negotiated settlement and allegation differ legally and are identified separately. Historical outcomes are included to explain institutional and financial context; they are not necessarily misconduct during a presidential term.


The New York civil-fraud appellate result must be described at its operative stage, including the vacated monetary award and any other surviving or disputed findings. Separate entries cover the Trump Foundation, Trump Organization tax case, Cohen and other proceedings. Avoid aggregating penalties that were vacated, settled, imposed or paid.

## Appendix C — Outstanding verification and distribution cautions

- A careful public release needs a lawyer/editor to review potentially defamatory assertions, original case dockets, pending appeals, and each subject’s known response.

- The 59-line officials/contractors roster is a sampling aid, not an all-administration census; the appointees disclosure database covers a much wider population.

- The source register and case matrix are searchable machine-readable companions; do not quote a research-lead hypothesis as a verified allegation.

- DOJ clemency register, court decisions, GAO audits, grants, contract modifications and public disclosures can change after October 31, 2026.
